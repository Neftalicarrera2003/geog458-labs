Lab 5: Maritime Traffic in Los Angeles

Study Area:
This project visualizes vessel movement in the Los Angeles, California area using AIS vessel trajectory data. The map is centered at longitude -118.24 and latitude 33.50, with a zoom level of 9 and a pitch of 35 degrees.

Data and Time Period:
The vessel trajectory data comes from my own AccessAIS CSV dataset. The CSV was processed into JSON files for visualization using Mapbox GL JS and deck.gl.

Date range: January 4, 2025 - January 6, 2025.

Map Interpretation:
There appears to be a significant amount of cargo vessel traffic moving in and out of Long Beach. Passenger ship traffic appears to be concentrated around Long Beach, the Port of Los Angeles, Seal Beach, and the Cabrillo Mole Terminal on Santa Catalina Island.

Tanker traffic appears less extensive, with vessel movements mainly occurring in and out of Long Beach. In comparison, the "Other" vessel category shows traffic across multiple ports and areas. This is likely because the category includes a broad range of vessel types.

Proposed Additional Feature:
One feature I would propose is the ability to view a vessel's name or identification number, its departure location, and its destination port. This would make it easier to understand where individual vessels are traveling and which ports they are arriving at.

Another useful feature would be adjustable animation speed. Being able to increase or decrease the timelapse speed would be a major quality of life improvement because the current animation feels too slow. This would allow users to explore vessel movements at a pace that works best for them.
