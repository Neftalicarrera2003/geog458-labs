# Lab 01: Geographic Research Note

## Place of interest
Seattle, Washington

## Geographic question
How does access to public parks vary across Seattle neighborhoods, and which communities may face greater limitations in nearby green space?

## Why this matters
Access to parks and public open space is an important part of urban health, recreation, and environmental quality. In a growing city like Seattle, differences in park access can reflect patterns of land use, neighborhood development, income, and historical planning decisions. Studying these differences helps reveal where residents may have fewer opportunities for outdoor activity, social gathering, and environmental relief.

## Research context
Seattle is a geographically diverse city with steep terrain, coastal access, and a mix of dense urban neighborhoods and more residential areas. Even within the same city, park access can vary considerably because of distance, population density, and infrastructure. This note focuses on how park availability differs across neighborhoods and whether some areas appear more underserved than others.

## Possible data
- Park boundaries and public open space polygons
- Neighborhood boundary files for Seattle
- Population and demographic data by neighborhood
- Street network or travel time layers for walking access
- Optional zoning or land-use data to interpret park distribution patterns

## Mapping idea
Create a web map that layers neighborhood boundaries with park locations and population counts. Then estimate park access by measuring distance or travel time from each neighborhood to nearby parks. This can be displayed as a map of park access, alongside a simple comparison of neighborhood-level park availability.

## Methodological approach
1. Collect neighborhood and park datasets for Seattle.
2. Join park and neighborhood information to calculate park coverage or park access by area.
3. Estimate the number of residents within a short walking distance of a public park.
4. Compare neighborhoods using accessibility measures such as distance to the nearest park or park area per resident.
5. Visualize results in an interactive web map and summarize key patterns in a short written analysis.

## Expected outcome
This analysis is likely to show that park access is uneven across Seattle, with some dense or lower-income neighborhoods potentially having fewer nearby green spaces than other parts of the city. The results may highlight the role of geography, land use, and urban planning in shaping access to public recreation and environmental amenities.

## Short conclusion
This project explores how digital mapping can support geographic inquiry into public space access. By combining spatial data and neighborhood-level analysis, it demonstrates how geography can be used to investigate social and environmental inequality in an urban setting.
